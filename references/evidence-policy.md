# Evidence Policy and Claim Firewall

Use this policy to prevent a seller's marketing narrative from becoming the agent's product truth.

## 1. Two Separate Questions

Always separate:

1. `What can this product objectively be inferred to be?`
2. `How is the seller choosing to present and sell it?`

The first question drives product structure, market-entry, differentiation, risk, and capability analysis. The second drives listing-quality, positioning, persuasion, and claim-audit analysis.

## 2. Evidence Classes

| Class | Meaning | Examples | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visible fact | Directly observable without trusting marketing copy | shape, connector, countable component, current displayed price, rating count | product baseline and listing snapshot |
| Seller claim | Statement controlled by seller or brand | title adjective, bullet, A+, spec table, infographic text, brand website | seller narrative and claim audit |
| External corroboration | Evidence not controlled by the listing and tied to the same model | certification database, independent manual, test report, regulator record | verify product capability |
| Customer report | User-generated experience | reviews, Q&A, forums | identify themes and challenge claims |
| Inference | Reasoning from evidence | likely primary job, positioning gap, possible return risk | analysis when logic and confidence are stated |
| Unknown | Unavailable, contradictory, or unverifiable | true material grade, measured waterproofing, actual battery life | information gap |

An observed statement is not the same as an observed product fact. It is valid to say `the listing claims 12-hour battery life`; it is invalid to say `battery life is 12 hours` without corroboration.

## 3. Objective First Pass

Before reading the title, secondary images, bullets, A+, description, comparison charts, specifications, or image copy:

1. Inspect only the first Amazon main image, expected to be the white-background product image.
2. Write a frozen baseline:
   - likely product type;
   - visible physical structure;
   - countable accessories;
   - visible interface and mounting method;
   - assembly or component relationships;
   - likely primary job;
   - ambiguous features;
   - unknown properties.

Do not use the title or later images to complete missing details in this baseline. Do not revise the baseline silently after reading marketing content. Record later changes as:

- `confirmed by external evidence`;
- `seller-claimed but unverified`;
- `contradicted`;
- `still unknown`.

## 4. Title Rules

Exclude the title from the objective first pass. Read it only after the first-image baseline is frozen.

Use the title to analyze:

- how the seller names the product;
- which identity, specification, compatibility, audience, and benefit claims the seller prioritizes;
- whether the title matches or stretches beyond what the first image shows.

All title content remains seller-authored. A neutral product name can help navigation and comparison after the baseline is complete, but it does not retroactively become first-pass evidence.

## 5. Image Rules

Use only the first main image for the objective first pass. Treat every secondary image as seller expression, including clean product views without text.

For secondary-image analysis:

- Text printed over an image is a seller claim.
- A dimension diagram is a seller-stated measurement.
- A cutaway rendering is an illustration, not proof of internal construction.
- A lifestyle scene indicates intended use or audience, not safe or effective use.
- A before/after graphic is a performance claim.
- Additional product angles can explain the seller's intended product presentation, but they do not alter the frozen first-image baseline.
- Packaging and accessory images are seller statements about inclusion until corroborated.

## 6. Seller Claim Firewall

Seller claims alone must not:

- increase product-success-variable scores;
- establish material, certification, safety, durability, waterproofing, capacity, compatibility, or performance;
- justify a premium price;
- prove differentiation or a technical barrier;
- establish suitability for a regulated or hazardous use;
- determine inventory, margin, or lifecycle decisions.

Seller claims may:

- reveal intended positioning and target audience;
- show which benefits the seller prioritizes;
- expose missing proof, contradictions, and overclaim risk;
- inform listing readability and persuasion analysis;
- generate hypotheses to verify externally;
- identify language competitors use in the market.

## 7. Corroboration Rules

Use stronger wording only when a claim is corroborated by an appropriate source tied to the same model.

Examples:

- Certification claim: verify in the certifier or regulator database.
- Material claim: use a technical sheet, manual, lab report, or reliable model-specific documentation.
- Compatibility claim: use official compatibility documentation and check exclusions.
- Performance claim: require a test method, independent test, or multiple consistent customer observations; still distinguish measured fact from anecdote.
- Included items: verify the selected child variation and packaging list.

The seller's own brand page is still a seller claim unless it links to independent evidence.

## 8. Customer Evidence Rules

Reviews are useful for falsification and expectation gaps:

- repeated leakage reports can challenge a waterproof claim;
- repeated missing-part reports can expose fulfillment or packaging issues;
- confusion about size can reveal image communication failure.

Reviews do not prove engineering specifications. State sample size, sampling method, recency, and whether the theme is repeated or anecdotal.

## 9. Mode Behavior

### Competitor Entry

Prioritize:

- objective product reconstruction;
- externally supported capabilities;
- unproven competitor claims;
- visible commodity versus defensible structure;
- market gaps and a minimum credible entry specification;
- what must be independently tested before copying or differentiating.

Treat bullets and A+ as `competitor messaging intelligence`, not ground truth.

### Owned Listing Audit

Compare:

- objective product baseline;
- seller claims;
- proof shown;
- customer feedback;
- consistency across title, images, bullets, A+, specifications, and variation.

Classify every major claim as:

- supported and well communicated;
- true but under-explained;
- seller-stated but unproven;
- contradicted or misleading;
- irrelevant or distracting.

### Dual

Produce both views, but never let listing polish increase the objective product score.

## 10. Wording Rules

Prefer:

- `第一张白底主图可见...`
- `仅凭第一张主图无法确认...`
- `第二张及后续图片试图表达...`
- `标题将其描述为...`
- `五点声称...，尚无外部佐证`
- `评论样本中重复出现...`
- `基于可见结构推断...`
- `无法确认...`

Avoid:

- `产品采用...` when only the seller says so;
- `该功能可以...` when only demonstrated in a render;
- `市场证明...` from one listing;
- `用户普遍认为...` without a defined review sample.
